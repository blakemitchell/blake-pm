This is my place to write, collect ideas, and experiment with the look and feel of a personal website.

## Where It Began

The site began with [Astro Sphere](https://github.com/markhorn-dev/astro-sphere), a theme by [Mark Horn](https://markhorn.dev). Its quiet, celestial design drew me in. The [original demo](https://astro-sphere-demo.vercel.app) shows the starting point; I have kept that attribution here as the site has changed.

## What Changed

The original theme grew into a small solar system of color palettes. Earth is the default, and the Atmosphere menu lets visitors choose another planet or Sagittarius A*. It also controls motion. An occasional flying saucer can change the palette, with an Undo button; after a visit, Pluto becomes available as a theme. Resetting preferences hides Pluto again until another visit.

The home page now has a curved horizon, aurora, field-notes artwork, stars, shooting stars, and a small cast of drifting objects. The [Debris Lab](/debris-lab) shows each object and explains how to spot one on the home page. The [Social page](/social) uses an orbit instead of a conventional list.

Writing now supports several post formats, including articles, short notes, links, photos, and reviews. The local visual editor lets me write posts and ordinary pages without a hosted CMS. The contact form and optional blog conversations are separate from the static pages.

## Built With

[Astro](https://astro.build) builds the pages, and [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com) hosts them. CSS and small pieces of JavaScript handle the visual effects. The visual editor is built with Keystatic. Blog conversations use EchoThread. The [source code is on GitHub](https://github.com/blakemitchell/blake-pm).
